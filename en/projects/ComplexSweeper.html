ComplexSweeper · Complex Mines

Four kinds of mines, 24 reachable values and a different way to reason about the board. Native HarmonyOS, Android and Apple ports of Qingyuexiao’s open-source game preserve its classic pixel style and circular complex-number mode.

Minesweeper with complex mines

+1−1+i−i

Four mine types

Positive and negative real and imaginary mines represent +1, −1, +i and −i. A cell shows the magnitude of the sum of all nearby mines.

Cancellation is not empty

A +1 mine and a −i mine give √2. A +1 and a −1 cancel to 0. A blank means there are no nearby mines; a zero can hide cancelling pairs.

Winning and expanding

Reveal every non-mine cell to win. Expansion requires the nearby flag count to match the actual mine count and the real-to-imaginary ratio to match the true ratio or its reciprocal. Flagged cells remain protected.

On phones, tap a covered cell to cycle flags and hold to reveal it. On macOS, left-click reveals and right-click flags. Select a revealed number to attempt expansion; select the face to restart. Three difficulties: 9×9 / 10 mines, 16×16 / 40 mines and 30×16 / 99 mines.

Apple

Swift + SwiftUI in one multiplatform project for iOS, iPadOS and macOS, with separate touch and mouse controls.

Source not yet public

All three ports use integer arithmetic and the mulberry32 generator. Board fingerprints for identical seeds check consistency between platforms. Only circular complex-number mode is ported; the upstream hyperbolic mode is not included.

Original game and ports

Qingyuexiao (Yueqing-Chen) created the original Zig + Win32 game, rules, atlas and audio; Rayawa maintains these three native ports. The ports are based on upstream v1.2.0 and their code is GPL-3.0. Original classic Minesweeper images belong to Microsoft; other additions and audio come from the upstream game. See each repository’s asset documentation for the specific licences.
